## When to stop (set 2026-11-25 09:55, before any paired result)

Lars, 2026-11-25: "Analyze eval outcomes as we go along and decide when we have enough evidence to make an educated
guess on the with/without names question." Claude's rule, applied after each paired part (A and B on the same cases and
model), on the mean per-case score difference B - A with its 95% bootstrap interval over cases:

- **Names matter**: the interval excludes 0 on a model with at least 28 paired cases (two parts). Run that model's
  remaining parts only if the sign flips between parts.
- **No practical difference**: the interval lies inside -0.05 to +0.05 on Sonnet with at least 28 paired cases. Then run
  one Opus part (14 cases) as a check; stop if Opus agrees (interval includes 0 and the mean is within 0.05).
- **No discrimination**: in both arms nearly every run scores 1.0 (at most 2 cases per part below 1.0 in either arm), so
  the suite cannot tell the arms apart; stop, and say the question needs harder cases.
- Otherwise run the next part, Sonnet first (more runs, cheaper), until all four parts are paired.
- **Budget**: Lars, 2026-11-25: "Try to stay under $100 total for the name/no-name evals." The runner sums every
  case's cost and starts no case that would take the total past $100. Sonnet measured about $0.85 a case at n=5 (first
  9 cases), so the plan is two Sonnet parts on both arms (about $48), then one Opus part on both arms, then more Sonnet
  only if money is left and the rule above has not ended the runs.

**Default when the difference is noise**: Lars, 2026-11-25: "If measured differences are within the noise, default to
no-names (except for named patterns, refactorings, smells, and so on, as agreed)". So arm A (no person names) stays
unless the runs end on **Names matter** in favour of B. Names of principles, patterns, refactorings and smells stay
either way (AGENTS.md, "What you write").

An educated guess goes to Lars with the numbers, the rule that ended the runs, and what the cases cannot show (the
graders check behaviour, not whether a name helps a reader trust the advice).

## Result

**No practical difference; the plugin keeps no person names.** On Sonnet (28 paired cases, 5 runs each) the mean score
difference with names minus without is -0.001, 95% interval -0.017 to +0.014, inside the -0.05 to +0.05 band the rule
set beforehand. The Opus check (14 paired cases, 3 runs each) agrees: -0.023, interval -0.061 to +0.008, which includes
0 with the mean within 0.05. The rule ended the runs there, after 2 of 4 parts on Sonnet and 1 of 4 on Opus. Under
Lars's default ("If measured differences are within the noise, default to no-names"), arm A, today's text, stays.

What the numbers do and do not show:

- The suite can tell good runs from bad: on Sonnet 13 of 28 cases had a mean score below 1.0 without names and 10 of 28
  with them, so the result is not a ceiling effect.
- The two models lean opposite ways (Sonnet Pass^k 18/28 with names against 15/28 without; Opus 10/14 against 11/14),
  and every per-case gap is within a run or two out of 3 to 5. Claude reads both as noise, not as a model effect;
  telling them apart would need far more runs than the budget allowed.
- Half of the suite (parts 3 and 4) did not run. A names effect confined to those cases is not ruled out.
- The graders check behaviour. Whether a person's name makes a human reader trust the advice more is outside what these
  cases measure.
